When to Surface This Information
Use this resource when a person may be at risk of self-harm, says they cannot stay safe, feels out of control, is in severe emotional distress, is alone and in danger, or may need urgent in-person help.

Use it when the person needs local human support in Sri Lanka rather than self-help alone.

Immediate Crisis Support
If you are in immediate danger or think you may harm yourself, seek urgent in-person help now. Go to the nearest hospital or emergency care setting as soon as possible.

If possible, do not stay alone. Ask a trusted person to stay with you, travel with you, or help you reach urgent care.

Mental Health Helplines
National Mental Health Helpline: 1926.

Sri Lanka’s mental health system is designed to provide care at primary, secondary, and tertiary levels, with services intended to be locally accessible.

Hospital and Local Care
If symptoms are severe, getting worse quickly, or you do not feel able to stay safe, go to a hospital. The policy states that hospital admission should be available when needed, and acute inpatient care is intended for people who cannot be safely managed at home.

The source also describes mental health outpatient clinics, outreach clinics, community support centres, and district-level services intended to support ongoing assessment, treatment, and continuity of care near home.

Reaching Out to Trusted People
If you are struggling but not in immediate physical danger, contact a trusted person now. This can be a family member, close friend, caregiver, or another reliable adult who can stay with you and help you get support.

The Sri Lanka mental health policy emphasizes community-based care with family and community participation, which supports involving trusted people when safety is a concern.

When to Go to a Hospital
Go to a hospital now if:
You feel unable to stop yourself from acting on self-harm thoughts
You are not safe being alone
You are severely distressed and cannot calm down
You are confused, overwhelmed, or losing control
You need urgent assessment because home support is not enough

The source states that acute wards should be able to manage people who are a significant risk to themselves or others.

Local Mental Health Care Pathways
Sri Lanka’s policy describes a district-based network that includes acute inpatient units, outpatient and outreach clinics, community support centres, day centres, resource centres, and rehabilitation or psychosocial care close to the community.

Most people with common mental health problems are intended to be treated in primary care, with more intensive hospital care used when needed.

Short Support Message
If you are in Sri Lanka and feel unsafe, call 1926 or go to the nearest hospital now. If you can, tell a trusted person immediately and do not stay alone.
